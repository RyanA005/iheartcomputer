How to Interview

Getting the interview

Getting a response to your application is unfortunately rare, but there are plenty of ways to stack your odds.

  • Write a good, tailored resume.
  • Keep your LinkedIn and portfolio up to date.
  • Apply to jobs you're actually qualified for.
  • Network, ask for referrals, and make connections.

Do the very best you can to represent yourself to an HR person or ATS system, larping is acceptable here*.

The interview is your opportunity

Initial applications are at the end of the day, out of your hands. Interviews are where you can actually represent yourself, hopefully to a real person.

  • You have an opportunity to show off your skills and personality.
  • Preparation, communication, and confidence make a difference.

As soon as you make it to the interview, your resume and LinkedIn matter dramatically less.

Interview basics

Don't mess up on the basics! Simple manners and little details will get you through even if you are not perfect.

In person

  • Arrive on time.
  • Dress appropriately.
  • Make eye contact.
  • Offer a handshake.

Online

  • Test your mic and camera.
  • Find a quiet, well-lit space.
  • Have a decent background.
  • Dress appropriately.

ALWAYS

  • Be polite, say please and thank you.
  • Stay focused, ask questions if you have them.
  • Speak clearly and be confident.
  • Be honest and direct.
  • Read the room*.

Types of interviews

Interviews in tech come in a few different flavors, all designed intentionally to assess something different.

Phone screen Quick introduction and basic qualifications
Behavioral Questions about past experiences
Technical Demonstrate your technical knowledge
Whiteboard / coding Solve problems while explaining your thinking
System design Design a solution and discuss tradeoffs
Practical / take-home Build or complete something realistic

The fun technical variants

These are the ones you actually study for.

  • Design this system.
  • Explain this concept.
  • Implement this algorithm.

A few tips.

  • Ask clarifying questions, understand the problem first.
  • Explain your process, think out loud to your interviewer.
  • Test, debug, and be ready to defend your solution.
